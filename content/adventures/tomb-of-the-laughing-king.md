## Overview
A classic dungeon crawl with a comic twist. **King Oswin the Merry** built his tomb as his final prank: every room is a puzzle or a trick, and the traps are designed to humiliate rather than kill (mostly). His loyal jester, **Tattercap**, was entombed with him and now haunts the tomb as a mischievous **poltergeist** (use Specter-like stats) who wants someone to finally "get the joke".

The prize: the king's **Crown of Mirth** and a treasury. The tomb's final secret is that the real treasure is a letter to his people — and a hidden hoard for whoever proves they understand the king's spirit (kindness and wit, not greed).

## Hook
In the village of Barrowby at the foot of Laughing Hill, **Sister Agathe** of the local shrine asks the hero to retrieve the **Crown of Mirth**, which she believes will stop the "laughing sickness": villagers are waking up giggling uncontrollably, and it's getting worse. She offers 50 gp. A rival treasure hunter, **Mags Ferrow**, is already heading up the hill with a crew.

## Scenes

### 1. The entrance
A stone door shaped like a grinning face with its tongue out. Inscription: "Knock, knock." Knocking causes a voice to ask "Who's there?" — the door opens for any joke that makes the voice laugh (Performance DC 12, or the player tells an actual joke — reward effort generously). Forcing the door (Athletics DC 18) triggers a bucket of glitter that clings for a week (disadvantage on Stealth).
- Mags Ferrow's crew's footprints lead in. One of them, **Dobbin**, sits outside with a broken leg and a sour mood; he'll share what he knows for a healing potion or kindness.

### 2. The Hall of Pratfalls
A long hall with a floor of alternating tiles painted with frowns and smiles. Stepping on a frown drops the hero into a pit of feathers (no damage; 5 minutes to climb out, and the tickling makes them laugh loudly — the tomb now knows they're here). Investigation DC 12: the smiles are safe, except the one with a tear.
- Three **skeletons** dressed as court musicians (SRD Skeleton, 13 HP) animate and play a march, then attack if the hero doesn't dance (Performance DC 11 to dance well enough that they applaud and let the hero pass).

### 3. The Riddle Gallery
Portraits of Oswin in silly poses. Each asks a riddle; answering three correctly opens the way (Intelligence DC 13 each, or the player solves them):
1. "What has keys but can't open locks?" (a piano)
2. "The more you take, the more you leave behind." (footsteps)
3. "What can fill a room but takes up no space?" (light — or laughter, which the portrait accepts with delight)
Wrong answers cause a portrait to squirt ink (Dexterity save DC 12 or blinded until the end of the next turn).

### 4. Mags Ferrow's crew
In the tomb's middle chamber, Mags (use Bandit Captain stats halved: AC 15, 26 HP, two scimitar attacks +5, 1d6+3) and one surviving thug (Bandit, 11 HP) are trapped in a giant birdcage, arguing. They beg to be let out.
- Freeing them: Mags may betray the hero later (Insight DC 14 reveals it), or be won over with a fair split (Persuasion DC 14).
- Leaving them: they escape later and ambush the hero near the treasury.

### 5. The Jester's Court
Tattercap the poltergeist appears: a flickering jester with bells that make no sound. He is the source of the laughing sickness — he's been sending jokes into people's dreams because he's lonely.
- He challenges the hero to make him laugh. Success (Performance or Deception DC 14, or genuinely funny roleplay) means he grants passage and reveals the king's secret.
- Failure or aggression: he attacks — AC 12, 22 HP, incorporeal, Tickling Touch +4 (2d6 psychic, and DC 12 Wisdom save or fall prone laughing until the end of the next turn). He vanishes at 0 HP but returns the next night unless the hero finds the letter.

### 6. The Tomb of King Oswin
A sarcophagus carved as a sleeping king with a whoopee cushion under his head. The **Crown of Mirth** rests on it — but it's a fake (a painted tin crown; Investigation DC 13). The real crown is in a secret compartment under the cushion, with **Oswin's letter**: "To whoever finds this — if you came for gold, here's a tin crown. If you came for my people, the real treasure is under the throne in Barrowby's old moot hall. Laugh often. — O."
- Taking the fake crown triggers the final prank: the room fills with (harmless) purple smoke and every skeleton in the tomb starts laughing.

## NPCs
- **Sister Agathe** — earnest, worried, not good with jokes.
- **Mags Ferrow** — rival treasure hunter; sharp, greedy, not cruel; can be redeemed.
- **Dobbin** — injured thug with a grudge against Mags.
- **Tattercap** — the jester's ghost; exuberant, sad underneath, wants his king's joke to be understood.
- **King Oswin** — never met, but present through inscriptions, portraits and his letter.

## Encounters
- Musician skeletons ×3 (avoidable by dancing).
- Mags Ferrow and her thug (roleplay or fight).
- Tattercap (roleplay or fight).
- Assorted harmless-but-embarrassing traps.

## Secrets & Clues
- The laughing sickness stops if Tattercap is laid to rest by someone who reads the king's letter aloud in his court.
- The real treasure (300 gp in old coins and the true **Crown of Mirth**, which lets the wearer cast the SRD spell *Hideous Laughter* once per day) is hidden in Barrowby itself.

## Possible Endings
- **The joke understood**: the hero reads the letter, Tattercap laughs until he fades into golden light, and the laughing sickness ends. The treasure in Barrowby is split between the hero and the village. Sister Agathe learns a joke.
- **The greedy ending**: the hero takes the tin crown and leaves. The sickness continues; the villagers are unimpressed.
- **Mags's ending**: if Mags betrays the hero and wins, she finds the tin crown — the epilogue can let the hero have the last laugh.
